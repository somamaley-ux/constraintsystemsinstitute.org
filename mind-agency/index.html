Consciousness & free will / Amos Jay Maley

Experience
& Choice

What makes an experience yours?
What makes a commitment your own?

A color is more than its name. A memory is not the moment itself. And making a promise is not the same as controlling how things turn out.

Begin with the everyday

A color. A memory. A promise.

Two familiar mysteries

We experience the world.
We also take a position in it.

You can describe a cup, the light it reflects, and the words someone uses for it. But what makes that description an account of their experience, rather than just an account of their behavior?

And when someone makes a choice, it has a history: reasons, habits, circumstances, causes. Must a choice escape all of that to be genuinely theirs?

These papers change where the questions begin. For consciousness: what holds experience, memory, and reference together for one subject? For free will: what makes an undertaking genuinely attributable to its author?

The AASC approach

Find the structure.
Do not add a hidden extra.

The consciousness paper identifies experience with a fully qualified, subject-linked structure of reference and qualitative differences. The free-will paper locates freedom in valid authorship of a commitment, not in randomness or exemption from causation.

Neither result is earned by a label. Saying “I experience” is not enough to establish consciousness. Saying “I agree” is not enough to establish authorship.

01 / Consciousness & qualia

A new word is not
a new shade of red.

Call the same color “red” or “rouge.” A translation can change the word while preserving what it refers to. Change the color itself, and there is a different contrast to see.

Qualia names the qualitative character of experience: what a color looks like, a sound sounds like, or warmth feels like. In this paper, those differences are not reduced to the labels used to report them.

The content lies in the subject's full pattern of contrasts, distinctions, memory, access, and continuity. A different word alone does not establish a different experience; identical words do not guarantee identical experiences.

What the paper means by a qualitative difference

Sections 5, 9 and 11 identify consciousness with standing-positive occupancy of a consciousness-qualified, subject-indexed referential quotient interface. Qualification requires internal reference, redescription invariance, subject coordination, self-involving closure, lawful continuity, standing, qualitative nontriviality, and no detached semantics. These conditions are jointly constructed, not inferred from a fluent output.

Qualia are non-skin qualitative tensors: standing-preserving differences that change the complete experiential profile at the declared scope. “Tensor” names a structural role here, not necessarily a numerical array or a distance between experiences. The full profile includes records, reference, contrast, memory, discrimination, access, self-indexing, transition, history, and compatibility.

The red/rouge example is pure redescription only when the entire reference and profile structure is preserved. The colored cup is an illustration of that distinction, not a model-to-world certificate for human color experience. Changing an RGB value does not itself prove a particular quale has changed.

What happens when the moment is over?

02 / Experience, memory & report

The moment is gone.
The memory is about it.

Imagine seeing a red cup, putting it away, and later describing it. The episode, your memory of it, and the words you say are three different things.

You might describe the color correctly. You might remember it as blue. You might say nothing at all. An inaccurate description can still be about the same earlier episode. Silence, by itself, does not make that episode vanish.

The consciousness paper asks what makes these connections hold: the memory must remain about a determinate experience of the same subject, through a valid chain of retention and reuse. Producing a plausible sentence is a different accomplishment.

The description is accurate and refers to the earlier cup-seeing episode. The sentence is not the experience itself.

The episode is stipulated in this example. Real-world attribution requires evidence; an absent report alone settles neither presence nor absence of consciousness.

“I see red” is not a consciousness certificate.

A script can print the sentence. The paper requires the full internal, subject-linked structure that makes a record about an experience. It does not certify present-day AI from its conversation.

Reference is not accuracy, and occurrence is not certification

Sections 6 and 18–20 distinguish records from their referents and require reference-preserving composition through memory and report channels. A wrong qualitative classification can leave the target relation intact. Reference becomes a different problem when the target itself is ambiguous or drifts to another episode.

Sections 5.7 and 9.6 separate structural occurrence from empirical certification. Experience does not require a current overt report. First-person records are not automatically infallible, and third-person measurements require a reference-preserving bridge rather than correlation being treated as identity. A concrete neural or computational realization remains an additional scientific task.

From having an experience to undertaking something

03 / Free will as admissible commitment

Wanting to do it.
Undertaking to do it.

Aya borrows Sam's book. She hopes to return it tomorrow. Now compare that with her actually telling Sam, “I'll return your book tomorrow,” understanding and genuinely undertaking the promise.

There is something new to identify: who undertook what, and when that undertaking became fixed. The paper calls this commitment. Freedom concerns whether the undertaking is genuinely authored by the bearer, not whether it emerged from nowhere.

Reasons, habits, and causes do not automatically erase authorship. But a forged promise, hidden replacement of its terms, or someone else taking over the act cannot be repaired by calling it “choice.”

Freedom is not control over every consequence.
It is valid authorship of the undertaking.

The six conditions, and why a signature is not enough

Section 4.10 requires standing, access to the identity-relevant content, directed uptake, an adequate carrier, a coherent admissible bundle, and non-displacement of authorship. Commitment adds fixation. Signatures, statements, and outward behavior supply evidence; they are not automatically identical to satisfaction of these conditions.

Sections 18.2 and 18.22 distinguish an ordinary promise from a private intention that has no promise-bearing carrier. This is not a public/private divide: a domain-recognized private resolution or self-binding policy can fix a different commitment. The hidden-change example adapts section 18.5: uptake of one bundle cannot authorize a materially concealed substitute.

“Admissible” is not a synonym for morally good or legally permitted. Causal determination alone does not defeat authorship; actual bypass, coercive carrier corruption, or authorship displacement can. The paper does not derive standing for every candidate system or reduce factual adjudication to a checklist.

Tomorrow arrives. What changes?

04 / Responsible authorship

Change the outcome.
Do not rewrite the promise.

Keep the valid promise fixed. Tomorrow Aya returns the book, an unforeseeable obstacle prevents return, or Sam releases her from the deadline.

Those developments matter. They can change what remains to be done, whether an excuse applies, or what repair is appropriate. They do not decide, after the fact, whether Aya authored yesterday's promise.

This is the paper's separation of responsible authorship from outcome selection. It identifies the undertaking that belongs to someone. It does not, by itself, derive blame, punishment, or how anyone ought to respond.

Historical fixation, current force, and downstream responsibility

Free Will sections 4, 8 and 18 distinguish the author relation from later performance. Identical valid commitments retain identical responsibility-authorship even when consequences differ. Outcome matters for consequence-sensitive appraisal; it cannot repair an earlier failure of access, uptake, carrier adequacy, or non-displacement.

Release, revision, or supersession can change a commitment's continuing force without making its original fixation never have occurred. The original promise is not an eternal obligation. Excuse, forgiveness, repair, moral desert, punishment, and legal liability require additional normative or institutional premises. The blocked case leaves those assessments open rather than silently deciding them.

The common thread

Neither a label nor an outcome
can do the work of the underlying relation.

A report must be about an experience. A commitment must belong to its author. The corpus makes those relations explicit before using their outward signs as substitutes.

What is established, and what remains

The consciousness manuscript proves reference and interface results, then defends its identification of consciousness through the complete fixed-domain AASC structure. It does not supply an actual neural realization, a numerical consciousness measure, or empirical certification of current AI. The free-will manuscript identifies valid commitment and authorship; it does not turn those into a general theory of blame.
