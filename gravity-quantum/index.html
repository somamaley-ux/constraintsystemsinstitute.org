01 / Kernel necessity and AMetric governance

Before a ruler or an equation,
what must stay definite?

A ruler assumes that we can keep track of what is being measured. An equation assumes that its symbols still refer to the same things after a calculation. AASC begins with the requirements that make such determinate use possible.

The Kernel establishes necessary roles for identity-preserving, non-degenerate construction: admissibility, standing, reference and irreversibility. The capstone instantiates those roles on actual physical use tasks and carries their governance into both dynamics branches.

The AMetric restriction comes before metric authority. A distance, rank, probability or hidden selector cannot simply be assumed to decide what counts as an admissible construction. Later descriptions must earn authority for their declared use.

What comes from the Kernel, and what comes later?

The necessity claim concerns fixed-domain, identity-preserving, non-degenerate construction. Its roles govern the later constructions; they do not alone choose a spacetime dimension, a physical action, a numerical mass or a Hamiltonian. Those are explicit data in the downstream physical constructors.

Here, emergence means structural dependency and derivation within those declared constructions. The order of this tour is an explanatory path through a branching proof, not a temporal creation story or a claim that every manuscript in the corpus has been formalized.

Related branch / Physical clocks

A clock belongs to the world.
It does not have to stand outside it.

This earlier manuscript branch explores physical clocks in its own constructed WDW setting. It is related reading, distinct from the capstone’s free-scalar receiving construction.

A sequence of spacetime slices can be labelled with a time coordinate. But a label is not yet a physical clock, and changing the slicing need not change the history.

The Wheeler-DeWitt branch follows this distinction all the way through: from an Einstein-source history, through its classical constraints, into a constructed quantum constraint system and a physical account of clocks and records.

The question becomes relational: what does one physical quantity record when another provides the clock? The final paper constructs three different clock descriptions and proves their agreement on their shared domain.

These limits distinguish a completed construction from a broader claim the papers do not make.
