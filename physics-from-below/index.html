Why the common foundation still matters when more physics is added

The Kernel argument applies to every non-degenerate determinate physical object and incidence, including objects never measured. A clock, a calibration, a propagation law and a more elaborate physical construction each incur the same four necessary functions. Their implementation can be implicit or distributed.

The new physical content of a branch specifies which comparisons and dynamics it supports. 02 · Special relativity

Build a ruler
from signals.

Before asking how far apart two things are, keep track of what happened: a signal left one endpoint, reached the other, and came back. Now build the comparison, one step at a time.

One chain of physical records

The signal must return to the same endpoint.

A departure, a reflection and a return belong to one recorded experiment. Clock readings and coordinates can be added, but they must keep referring to those events.

E1 → E2 → E3. The event identities stay fixed throughout.
  1. E1 · departure at AThe signal leaves.
  2. E2 · reflection at BThe signal turns back.
  3. E3 · return to AThe same clock records its return.

What stays fixed: the endpoints A and B, the three identified events, and the record connecting them.

Step 1 of 5
  1. Keep the events. Identify the same emission, reflection and reception before attaching numbers.
  2. Read the clock. A calibrated clock at A records emission at 0 microseconds and return at 2 microseconds.
  3. Compare distance. For this stationary, delay-free round trip, radar distance is c × (return − departure) / 2 = 299.8 metres. The reflection is assigned the midpoint time, 1 microsecond.
  4. Map possible reach. The paper uses a complete family of admissible propagation comparisons, not this one ray alone. With its completed causal order, clock and isotropy conditions, it constructs a spatial comparison and a finite round causal cone.
  5. Change observer. Preserving that cone and one common rest-clock calibration gives the Lorentz frame law. At relative speed 0.6c, the same departure and return are 2.5 microseconds apart in the moving network; A’s clock still records 2 microseconds.
What the reconstruction uses

The homogeneous comparison branch supplies four dimensions and composition of displacement comparisons. Theorem 5.2 constructs its affine structure. Completed causal order, clock comparison and rest-comparison properties give a finite bound (Proposition 5.6); full boundary isotropy constructs the spatial quadratic form (Theorem 5.7). A common rest-clock calibration then removes the remaining scale freedom, giving the Lorentz law (Theorem 6.5 and Corollary 6.6). Actual repeatable signals and endpoint records are needed for the clock and radar experiment; the cone alone does not supply a device.

This illustration uses c = 299,792,458 metres per second and a stationary reflector with no reflection delay. The final overlay uses a frame moving at 0.6c. It shows a time axis and simultaneity direction, not a new physical signal.

For an actual controlled push from rest to 0.60c, the impulse is 224.8 MN·s. In the paper, the small-speed ratio of delivered impulse to velocity change measures inertia; conservation and the source’s Lorentz response force that coefficient to equal E₀/c². A coordinate change alone does not supply this experiment.

What the effects assume, and where they are proved

The clock is an ideal calibrated inertial device on a valid run. Length uses stable endpoints and the measuring frame’s synchronized equal-time rule. These are exact flat-target comparisons; curved regions and accelerated devices require their additional conditions. The duration law is Theorem 7.6 (p. 39); time dilation, length contraction and simultaneity are Theorems 9.1, 9.2 and 9.4 (pp. 42–43).

Rest-energy equivalence also requires dynamics: a complete attached total source, finite conserved charges, a stable rest branch, an active Lorentz action and controlled impulse records ending in the same internal state. Theorem 11.9 (p. 52) derives the inertial response and E₀ = mᵢc² without assuming a mass shell. The controls illustrate these results within those conditions.

05 / Bring the descriptions together

The same world has to
survive every description.

Recovering a familiar equation is one part of the task. The next is to establish how the descriptions belong together.

A spacetime account and a quantum account must keep their connection to the physical source they describe. Where their uses overlap, their records and allowed changes have to agree.

This is the structural unification developed by AASC: a common foundation, constructed physical sources, and compatible descriptions whose connection is preserved through their use.

What the common-source construction establishes

The special-relativity manuscript gives two connected routes: construction of the Lorentzian form from completed physical comparisons, and its matching to the metric and continuation of an already occupied common source. Corollary 12.7 relates Einstein, Schrödinger and Lorentz-path descriptions through a common history. Theorems 15.9–15.10 supply a joint fixed-background example with actual clock, signal and response records.

In that example, the Einstein projection has a vacuum source. The positive laboratory response and quantum-state evolution remain attached to their own physical uses. Proposition 15.11 proves the obstruction to treating that laboratory response as the same complete gravitational source on unchanged flat geometry. The coupled Wheeler–DeWitt and Standard Model constructions have their own source identifications and domains (§§15.3–15.4).

The Einstein result fixes the minimal local first-curvature response in its stated Lorentzian realization. The Schrödinger result fixes the closed scalar nonrelativistic normal form under its state-space, domain and calibration conditions. The tour follows those particular recoveries and their common-source connections.
