01 / The deeper starting point

If spacetime needs explaining,
what can physics start with?

We picture things living in space and changing through time. But what makes something a definite physical thing in the first place?

It must be this object, with these relations and these possible changes. To follow it through change, we must establish what makes it the same object. A description has to answer to what physically obtains of that object.

AASC takes these as necessary features of physical reality. They apply to an unobserved object just as much as to a laboratory measurement. The argument begins here, before supplying a spacetime metric: the structure that assigns distances and durations.

The starting point is what makes a physical world definite.
Geometry must then earn its place as a description of that world.

Reference

What makes this
the same thing?

Faithful descriptions keep track of which object they concern, and whether a change preserves its identity.

Standing

What actually
obtains of it?

A relation or proposed use must qualify under the actual conditions. Giving it a name cannot make it qualify.

Admissibility

Can these things
fit together?

A combination must preserve the identities, relations and conditions that give its parts physical meaning.

Irreversibility

Which occurrence
was it?

A later change cannot replace the original occurrence. This holds even for systems with reversible dynamics.

These four functions form AASC’s Kernel. The necessity argument says that removing any of them loses part of the definite physical content one was claiming. Every richer construction, including a clock, a geometry or a physical law, incurs the same requirements.

Why the AMetric boundary matters

A calculation cannot create
its own physical authority.

The Kernel leads to the AMetric boundary: a constraint on which distinctions, selections and descriptions can qualify. A description cannot supply a physical distinction that its starting point does not support. A later successful calculation cannot decide which original object or event it was supposed to describe.

This boundary uses no primitive distance, order, probability or passage of time. The physical constructions governed by it can then establish persistence, connections and ordered change before receiving a numerical geometry.

“Before” means an order of dependence. This is about what spacetime descriptions require, rather than an earlier era of the universe.

How fixation and bivalence fit into this starting point

Fixation keeps the physical subject and conditions of the question fixed. Bivalence says that, for that complete, evaluated question, the proposed construction qualifies or it does not. Whether an observer can decide which is a separate matter.

AASC permits many faithful descriptions of the same physical content. Their vocabulary or coordinates can differ, but they cannot silently substitute another object, change the question, or erase a physical distinction. The foundational uniqueness argument is developed in the Foundations tour.

The AMetric boundary and the richer premetric physical structures do different work. A sequence of connected events already has physical ordering; the boundary itself does not supply a primitive time order.

Source: Special Relativity from AASC, §§1–3 and §16.1; Theorem 2.1 and Proposition 2.4. The foundational papers are linked in the Foundations research section.

02 / Where the theories connect

One physical source.
Different questions about it.

If spacetime geometry and a quantum state describe physical reality, what ties them to the same reality?

Sharing a foundational vocabulary is the first connection. AASC goes further by constructing a common physical source and history, then showing how its spacetime and quantum descriptions preserve their connection to it.

Here, “source” means the physical system with its actual state, relations and allowed changes. Its descriptions inherit their physical meaning from that common construction. Their connection has to survive as the system changes.

A common starting label is insufficient. If a source change has a spacetime description and a quantum description, their respective evolution rules must still describe that same change. The common-history construction supplies this compatibility on its stated physical domains.

Special relativity joins here

Whose events? Whose clocks?

The relativistic comparisons must concern the events, clock readings and motion of that same physical source. The paper constructs this connection as well as its route to Lorentzian geometry.

The Standard Model arc joins here

What persists through the responses?

The matter and interaction roles are reconstructed through a common persistence bearer: what retains its identity while supporting distinct matter, gauge and Higgs responses.

This is AASC’s structural approach to unification: establish the common physical content, then construct the connected forms in which it appears.

The papers establish these connections for specified physical constructions. Each branch retains the conditions that support its geometry, state space and dynamics.

Which common-source connections the papers establish

The special-relativity manuscript gives two connected routes: construction of the Lorentzian form from completed physical comparisons, and its matching to the metric and continuation of an already occupied common source. Corollary 12.7 relates Einstein, Schrödinger and Lorentz-path descriptions through a common history. Theorems 15.9–15.10 supply a joint fixed-background example with actual clock, signal and response records.

In that example, the Einstein projection has a vacuum source. The positive laboratory response and quantum-state evolution remain attached to their own physical uses. Proposition 15.11 proves the obstruction to treating that laboratory response as the same complete gravitational source on unchanged flat geometry. The coupled Wheeler–DeWitt and Standard Model constructions have their own source identifications and domains (§§15.3–15.4).

The Einstein result fixes the minimal local first-curvature response in its stated Lorentzian realization. The Schrödinger result fixes the closed scalar nonrelativistic normal form under its state-space, domain and calibration conditions. These common-source results do not assert a universal interacting quantum-gravity law.

04 / Worked example: special relativity

Build a ruler
from signals.

Now follow one branch in more detail. The departure, reflection and return keep their physical identities while clock comparison, propagation structure and calibration establish their spacetime description. The familiar relativistic results are the endpoint of this construction.

One chain of physical records

The signal must return to the same endpoint.

A departure, a reflection and a return belong to one recorded experiment. Clock readings and coordinates can be added, but they must keep referring to those events.

E1 → E2 → E3. The event identities stay fixed throughout.
  1. E1 · departure at AThe signal leaves.
  2. E2 · reflection at BThe signal turns back.
  3. E3 · return to AThe same clock records its return.

What stays fixed: the endpoints A and B, the three identified events, and the record connecting them.

Step 1 of 5
  1. Keep the events. Identify the same emission, reflection and reception before attaching numbers.
  2. Read the clock. A calibrated clock at A records emission at 0 microseconds and return at 2 microseconds.
  3. Compare distance. For this stationary, delay-free round trip, radar distance is c × (return − departure) / 2 = 299.8 metres. The reflection is assigned the midpoint time, 1 microsecond.
  4. Map possible reach. The paper uses a complete family of admissible propagation comparisons, not this one ray alone. With its completed causal order, clock and isotropy conditions, it constructs a spatial comparison and a finite round causal cone.
  5. Change observer. Preserving that cone and one common rest-clock calibration gives the Lorentz frame law. At relative speed 0.6c, the same departure and return are 2.5 microseconds apart in the moving network; A’s clock still records 2 microseconds.
What the reconstruction uses

The homogeneous comparison branch supplies four dimensions and composition of displacement comparisons. Theorem 5.2 constructs its affine structure. Completed causal order, clock comparison and rest-comparison properties give a finite bound (Proposition 5.6); full boundary isotropy constructs the spatial quadratic form (Theorem 5.7). A common rest-clock calibration then removes the remaining scale freedom, giving the Lorentz law (Theorem 6.5 and Corollary 6.6). Actual repeatable signals and endpoint records are needed for the clock and radar experiment; the cone alone does not supply a device.

For an actual controlled push from rest to 0.60c, the impulse is 224.8 MN·s. In the paper, the small-speed ratio of delivered impulse to velocity change measures inertia; conservation and the source’s Lorentz response force that coefficient to equal E₀/c². A coordinate change alone does not supply this experiment.

What the effects assume, and where they are proved

The clock is an ideal calibrated inertial device on a valid run. Length uses stable endpoints and the measuring frame’s synchronized equal-time rule. These are exact flat-target comparisons; curved regions and accelerated devices require their additional conditions. The duration law is Theorem 7.6 (p. 39); time dilation, length contraction and simultaneity are Theorems 9.1, 9.2 and 9.4 (pp. 42–43).

Rest-energy equivalence also requires dynamics: a complete attached total source, finite conserved charges, a stable rest branch, an active Lorentz action and controlled impulse records ending in the same internal state. Theorem 11.9 (p. 52) derives the inertial response and E₀ = mᵢc² without assuming a mass shell. The controls illustrate these results within those conditions.
