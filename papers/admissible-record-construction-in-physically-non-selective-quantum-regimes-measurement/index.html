Author’s publisher description for this release
A Kernel-Role Determinacy Theorem for Record Fixation, Fixed-Domain Quotients, and Boundary-Trace Normal Form This paper specializes the AASC kernel to the construction and use of quantum measurement records. Its central question is not initially how outcomes are produced, whether a wavefunction collapses, or which interpretation of quantum theory is correct. It asks what must be fixed for a measurement record to function as the same determinate physical and evidential object across retention, prospective use, deliberation, comparison, and report. For any non-degenerate determinate physical object, state, carrier, transformation, record, comparison, or endpoint, the paper takes the governing kernel to be: NDObj(x) ⇒ K(x) = (Adm, Stand, Ref, Irr). Admissibility, standing, reference, and irreversibility are therefore treated as necessary role conditions of determinate record use, not as an optional interpretive overlay added to an otherwise complete physical description. Measurement-record determinacy The manuscript constructs a fixed-domain measurement-record target in which a record must preserve: reference to the same construction token; standing-bearing evidential eligibility; admissible reuse; original act-time qualification; lawful redescription; retained and prospective record use; deliberative and comparative use; reportability and downstream inheritance. The resulting assignment space is quotiented by equivalence of standing-bearing measurement-record content. A rival quotient claiming exactly the same content collapses to the canonical measurement-record quotient. Representational differences that preserve the complete role content are treated as skin; differences affecting record identity, evidential ordering, admissibility, continuation, or downstream use remain physically relevant. Physically non-selective quantum regimes The paper then studies a physically non-selective quantum regime containing unitary branching, entanglement, decoherence, branch-local records, and stable local reports. The central result is conditional and target-specific: The bare unitary-entangling-decohering package can support multiple stable branch-local records without supplying a unique standing evidential record for the fixed construction-relative target. This is not a claim that branch-local records are unreal, unstable, or physically useless. It is a distinction between: physical occurrence of branch-local record structures; local branch-relative reportability; evidential role fixation for a record reused as the same standing object across the measurement practice. The paper shows that non-selective structures may agree on branch weights, density content, decoherence, correlations, instrument sums, channel closure, effect weights, projector orthogonality, and repeatability without selecting one fine record as the standing evidential record. Fixed-domain quotient and fixation commutation The theorem ladder establishes: fixed-domain assignment-space forcing; exact quotient construction for standing-bearing record content; same-scope equivalence collapse; fixation commutation under admissible transformation; inheritance of the original qualification requirement; persistence-bearing reuse; asymmetry exhaustion; anti-weakening; selector exhaustion. If evidential ordering varies across admissible assignments while the target requires one fixed-domain ordering, the ordering is not defined on that scope merely because numerical values or branch-local reports happen to coincide. The paper therefore treats measurement-record fixation as prior to the theory-level evidential reuse that depends on it. Boundary-trace normal form After internal and provenance/indexical routes have been exhausted, the residual same-domain route is a singleton stable boundary trace. Such a trace is not collapse dynamics, hidden-variable selection, or a new physical mechanism. It is an admissibility-preserving role anchor that can fix a record-bearing assignment only when its channel, incidence, transport law, original authority, and singleton-positive condition are independently warranted. The result distinguishes: sound partial selection from complete role fixation; unique trace value from unique original occupant; class-valued record fixation from raw-token fixation; later discovery of an original qualification from retroactive repair; physical record occurrence from standing evidential identity. Finite quantum specialization The paper occupies the abstract structure with an explicit finite two-branch quantum measurement domain containing: two outcomes; two fine records and one merged record; denominator-two branch weights; a balanced integer-amplitude state; density-table realization; selective and non-selective instrument maps; environment-record maps; projective branch maps; complementary effects; zero-product projectors; repeatability operations. On the declared balanced witness, amplitude weights, density weights, branch-map traces, effect weights, pointer records, instrument records, environment records, and channel records agree on the same branch architecture. Selective surfaces preserve the fine-record distinction. Non-selective surfaces agree only on the merged record. The fine records therefore remain separated, while the declared non-selective surfaces supply no singleton fine-record selector. The finite selector-boundary theorem eliminates the disjunction of candidate hidden selectors across the state, instrument, environment, channel, effect, and repeatability surfaces. The resulting diagnosis is precise: the missing structure is admissible fine-record role fixation, not further branch production, weighting, decoherence, stabilization, or repeatability. Scope and non-claims The manuscript establishes a finite, conditional, manuscript-facing theorem. It does not claim: to derive Hilbert-space quantum mechanics; to derive general POVM theory; to derive full projection-valued-measure theory; to reconstruct the full operator algebra; to provide a general Kraus representation theorem; to reconstruct complete positivity; to derive the continuum Born rule; to derive universal physical decoherence theory; to introduce a new collapse dynamics; to prove that no physical collapse process occurs in nature; to prove physical branch selection; to establish interpretive truth; to classify every possible physical measurement mechanism. The absence of a hidden selector on the declared finite non-selective surfaces is therefore not a theorem that no physical collapse or selection process can occur elsewhere. It is the finite theorem that those declared surfaces do not themselves supply the required fine-record selector. Relation to quantum interpretations The result does not deny the practical success of quantum theory, decoherence, branch-relative reporting, instrumental updates, or decision-theoretic methods. A practice aimed only at prediction, branch-local reporting, or instrumental updating may be a different target from the construction-relative record use studied here. If a theory claims to underwrite retained, prospective, deliberative, and evidential reuse of the same record-bearing construction token, it must preserve the relevant kernel roles and admit a standing-bearing fixation regime. Bare Everettian dynamics, without additional dynamics, ontological privileging, or admissible restriction to one constructional class, supplies no fine-record selector on the declared target. A strategy that preserves non-selective physics while restricting admissible record-fixation assignments belongs to a restriction-to-one-constructional-class route. Instrumentalist and QBist strategies instead reduce or redirect the target explanatory burden. Role in the AASC programme This paper provides the record-fixation layer of the AASC Quantum Determinacy Pair. Its companion Bell-scope work addresses the compatibility and no-signaling side of quantum witness determinacy. Together, the pair treats quantum records and witnesses as kernel-governed standing structures rather than as automatically determinate physical occurrences. The paper’s principal contribution is to identify the first failure point in a physically non-selective measurement regime: The initial problem is not necessarily the production of branch-local outcomes. It is the admissible fixation of the record role through which outcomes are retained, reported, remembered, compared, and used as evidence. The public Lean 4 formalization is synchronized with the finite measurement-boundary release v0.9.1 and provides the finite artifact for the declared effect, projector, compatibility, closed-stack, boundary-certificate, and audit layers. Complete publisher description
A Kernel-Role Determinacy Theorem for Record Fixation, Fixed-Domain Quotients, Finite Measurement-Boundary Compatibility, and Boundary-Trace Normal Form This manuscript develops a fixed-domain AASC analysis of the quantum measurement problem centered on admissible record-role fixation rather than outcome production alone. Its governing claim is that AASC is not an optional interpretive overlay on physics: once a physical record is asserted as a non-degenerate determinate object under fixed-domain use, admissibility, standing, reference, and irreversibility are already active as minimal necessity conditions of objecthood. The paper studies physically non-selective quantum regimes, especially Everettian branching structures, in which multiple stable post-measurement records are physically realized without a privileged selector fixing one record as the standing evidential continuation for downstream reuse. It distinguishes physical record occurrence from the stronger requirement that a record stand as the same evidential object across retained reuse, prospective reuse, deliberation, comparison, and theory-level confirmation. The central theorem establishes that once measurement records are required to support standing-bearing downstream use, admissible record-fixation assignments are structurally forced. The manuscript proves that admissible record-fixation assignments form a forced same-domain realization space, standing-bearing evidential content factors through a unique measurement-record quotient, alternative same-scope equivalence relations collapse, fixation commutation is necessary for theory-level evidential stabilization, and unresolved admissible multiplicity yields assignment-relative evidential lineage rather than construction-relative evidential determination. A major addition in this version is an explicit finite quantum measurement construction integrated into the manuscript body. The finite development defines a two-branch measurement carrier with fine and merged records, denominator-two weights, a balanced amplitude witness, density numerator tables, selective and non-selective instruments, environment records, projective branch maps, finite effects, zero-product projector/repeatability structure, and cross-surface compatibility. It proves that record, pointer, density, instrument, channel, effect, projector/repeatability, decoherence, Born-weight, and coarse-record surfaces agree on the declared finite branches while no declared non-selective surface supplies a hidden fine-record selector. The associated Lean 4 formalization has been synchronized through release v0.9.1. It verifies the finite measurement-boundary stack over declared carriers and hypotheses, including the corrected zero-effect/projector layer, finite selector-boundary facts, closed-stack endpoint, and audit manifest. The formalization is finite, conditional, and manuscript-facing: it does not claim to derive full Hilbert-space quantum mechanics, general POVM/PVM theory, full operator algebra, physical decoherence theory, the continuum Born rule, physical selection, interpretive truth, or a physical no-collapse theorem. The manuscript further develops asymmetry-source exhaustion for admissible record-fixation, Everettian asymmetry exhaustion, fail-closed fixation conditions, selector-exhaustion for interpretation packages, and boundary-trace fixation as the residual admissible normal form. A persistence-bearing continuation bridge connects measurement-record fixation to realized continuation loci and standing-bearing downstream reuse. Under this interpretation, measurement records are not merely abstract evidential objects but persistence-bearing continuation anchors within admissible fixed-domain lineage structure. The result is not a collapse dynamics, hidden-variable theory, or new physical law. Its claim is structural: physically non-selective multiplicity, branch differentiation, normalized weighting, coherence removal, repeatability, effect completeness, and cross-surface agreement do not by themselves supply admissible fine-record role fixation. A complete same-domain account of measurement-record determinacy must therefore occupy an admissible fixation locus. This paper is the record-side member of the paired AASC quantum determinacy arc. Its companion Bell-witness manuscript treats Bell-nonlocal quantum entanglement as boundary-level compatibility: the present paper fixes the standing record object, while the Bell-side theorem fixes the reusable Bell witness sampled by such records.
