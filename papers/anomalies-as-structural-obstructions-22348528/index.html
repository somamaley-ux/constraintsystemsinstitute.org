About this paper

Quantum field theory uses the word “anomaly” for several physically distinct phenomena: failure of a dynamical gauge symmetry at the quantum level, anomalous nonconservation of a meaningful global current, obstruction to gauging a global symmetry, topology-sensitive determinant phases, mixed-background noninvariance, bulk–boundary inflow, ultraviolet–infrared matching constraints, and renormalized trace identities.

This volume develops a fixed-target classification of these phenomena within the Standard Model symmetry programme. It distinguishes their physical loci, causal ancestry, obstruction types, repair mechanisms, presentation freedoms, and target-update boundaries rather than collapsing them into a single exact/broken or conserved/nonconserved label.

The analysis is formulated under the Admissibility–Standing–Reference–Irreversibility kernel. A determinate anomaly claim already requires fixed identity, lawful comparison and transformation, consequence-bearing use, and irreversible fixation. Conventional anomaly theory then supplies the target-specific currents, measures, determinant phases, descent classes, index data, global obstructions, inflow relations, matching conditions, and renormalized operator identities.

Scope and dependencies

The result does not claim that anomaly cancellation uniquely derives the Standard Model, fixes numerical couplings, or determines a unique infrared phase. It is complete only for the declared target and constructor grammars, and the executable audit layer does not rederive imported continuum results such as determinant-line theory, bordism classification, or renormalized quantum-field-theoretic theorems.

The volume establishes a common anomaly grammar distinguishing: gauge inconsistency from anomalous global response; local descent from global determinant and bordism obstruction; consistent, covariant, and Bardeen-shifted current representatives; counterterm redistribution from genuine physical cancellation; anomaly inflow from retrospective repair; ’t Hooft anomaly matching from uniqueness of infrared realization; trace anomalies from gauge anomalies; closed anomaly targets from generalized, higher-group, non-invertible, and global-form frontier interfaces. Public anomaly coordinates and repair claims are admitted only when they preserve the fixed target and its invariant physical content.
