Beyond Purity Loss

Source-Accounted Quantum Dynamics and False-Well Retention

Amos Jay Maley · 49 pages

Read paperDownload PDFZenodo work DOI

Does losing quantum coherence mean a system is more likely to remain in a false well? This paper separates that question from the production of entanglement and from the energy that sources classical geometry.

Its principal theorem proves local high-regularity existence and initial entanglement for a restricted finite quantum system–bath model with deterministic expectation-sourced classical geometry and dynamical classical references. It does not establish late-time false-vacuum protection or a cosmological decay rate.

An editorial overview of the author's published paper. The linked PDF contains the full definitions, proofs, figures and references.

One declared source-accounted evolution

The construction retains matter, bath, interaction, counterterm and reference contributions in the coupled equations. Its principal common-profile theorem treats finitely many unbounded quantum coordinates sharing a fixed spatial profile, with equal positive kinetic normalization, a strictly coercive quartic potential, compatible Einstein constraints and an invertible classical reference chart.

A fixed-finite extension permits distinct spatial profiles under additional injectivity and Gram-gap conditions. A separate expanding homogeneous model has a global forward continuation theorem under its own assumptions. Neither extension establishes vacuum stabilization.

Purity loss is not protection

On a prescribed expanding background, all three tested full-coupling arms of the positive-quartic experiment have lower time-averaged false-well retention than the bare control over the tested interval. A small intermediate-coupling improvement relative to the counterterm-only control coexists with that worse-than-bare result.

These are finite-time regional occupation comparisons, not first-passage survival probabilities or vacuum lifetimes. The initial packet includes above-barrier energy, and the experiments do not simulate the theorem's fully coupled Einstein/reference evolution. The predeclared primary comparison is full minus counterterm-only; full minus bare remains a separate contrast.

Derived dynamics, not an inserted decoherence rate

For the prescribed-background model, the paper derives an exact finite-cutoff projection-memory identity and a second-order time-convolutionless approximation from the actual two-time bath correlations. It separates noise and response contributions without imposing a Markov, secular or thermal-equilibrium assumption.

A parity-based theorem bounds the approximation error at fourth order in weak coupling at each fixed finite matrix cutoff. Numerical coupling-halving comparisons follow that order. At stronger coupling the approximation develops negative eigenvalues and loses positivity, exposing its failure rather than establishing a positive constant decay rate.

What follows, and what does not

The mathematical conclusions follow from the explicit equations and stated model assumptions; they do not require acceptance of AASC. AASC motivates the source-accounting questions, while identification with the intended upstream physical source remains a separate issue.

The deterministic mean-source evolution does not provide self-consistent stochastic-gravity closure. No cutoff-uniform unbounded-model error bound, interacting quantum-field limit, irreversible decoherence theorem, positive Markov semigroup or protection of the observed universe is claimed.

An appendix separately identifies a printed free-bath mode that does not satisfy its stated differential equation. That correction is logically independent of the construction and is not a blanket refutation of the published physical proposal.

Beyond Purity Loss

Source-Accounted Quantum Dynamics and False-Well Retention

Overview

This paper develops a mathematically controlled finite quantum system–bath model with dynamically sourced classical geometry and classical reference fields. It investigates the relationship between entanglement, reduced-purity loss, energy exchange, decoherence, and false-well retention.

The central result is deliberately bounded. The paper proves local high-regularity existence and initial entanglement for a restricted class of interacting quantum–classical-reference models. It does not claim to establish late-time false-vacuum protection, a cosmological decay rate, irreversible decoherence, or stabilization of the observed universe.

The construction begins with an explicitly declared finite quantum Hamiltonian, classical reference action, expectation-sourced Einstein equations, and reference-gauge equations. Matter, bath, interaction, counterterm, and reference contributions are retained in one source ledger rather than treating reduced noise or decoherence kernels as complete gravitational sources.

Principal theorem

The main theorem establishes local existence, uniqueness, and continuous dependence for a finite common-profile anharmonic quantum/reference system under explicit hypotheses, including:

  • finitely many unbounded quantum coordinates;

  • a common spatial profile;

  • equal positive kinetic normalization;

  • strictly coercive quartic interaction;

  • compatible Einstein constraint data;

  • admissible Lorentzian and ADM margins;

  • an invertible dynamical classical reference chart;

  • normalized pure quantum initial data.

On a sufficiently short interval in the intrinsic reference-clock coordinate, the system admits a unique strong solution satisfying:

  • the Schrödinger equation;

  • the unreduced expectation-sourced Einstein equation;

  • the classical reference equations;

  • the declared gauge conditions;

  • quantitative lower-energy continuous dependence;

  • recovery of the subsidiary constraints.

The proof combines weighted quantum propagation, positive reference-gauge elimination, differentiated source estimates, a coupled contraction argument, and subsidiary-constraint recovery.

A separate fixed-finite multi-profile extension allows distinct spatial profiles under additional injectivity, Gram-gap, coercivity, and boundedness assumptions. It does not provide a field-cutoff limit or an unrestricted interacting field theory.

Initial entanglement

For an admissible quartic system–bath potential and pure product initial data with nonzero position variances, the paper derives a strictly negative initial curvature of the reduced purity in intrinsic reference time whenever the interaction is nonzero.

This establishes short-time entanglement directly from the coupled Hamiltonian. It does not establish:

  • irreversible monitoring;

  • persistent decoherence;

  • improved false-well survival;

  • a universal decay rate;

  • a cosmological lifetime;

  • suppression of tunnelling at late times.

Reduced-purity loss and regional retention are treated as distinct observables throughout the paper.

Source-accounted geometry and operational references

The paper emphasizes that a reduced noise kernel is not automatically a complete gravitational source. The full source ledger retains:

  • quantum matter energy;

  • bath energy;

  • interaction energy;

  • counterterm contributions;

  • classical reference-field energy;

  • metric and reference responses;

  • exchange terms required by the coupled equations.

The reference sector is not treated as free external bookkeeping. It is represented by an independent dynamical classical action with its own stress tensor and equations of motion. The operational construction also includes finite metric probes, reference-chart transport, Ramsey-type response protocols, and explicit reference exchange.

Within the conditional AASC interpretation, this provides a concrete source-standing and operational-response realization. The mathematical existence and purity theorems themselves, however, follow from the explicit model equations and do not require acceptance of AASC as an axiom.

False-well retention experiments

The paper separates several finite models rather than transferring conclusions from one model to another.

A harmonic-bath experiment on a prescribed expanding background compares:

  • full system–bath coupling;

  • counterterm-only evolution;

  • bare evolution.

The full false-packet model retains more probability than the counterterm-only control in the stated experiment, but less than the bare control. These are finite-time regional occupation comparisons, not first-passage survival probabilities or vacuum lifetimes.

A positive-quartic system–bath experiment provides an actual coercive double-well model. Across the tested full-coupling arms, time-averaged regional retention is lower than in the bare control over the specified interval. A small intermediate-coupling improvement relative to the counterterm-only control coexists with this worse-than-bare result.

The paper therefore does not support the general claim that entanglement or decoherence protects a false vacuum. It shows instead that retention depends on the complete interaction, preparation, counterterm, geometry, time window, and observable.

The initially above-barrier packet used in the positive-quartic experiment is explicitly not treated as a clean tunnelling preparation.

Global homogeneous continuation

A separate homogeneous finite-mode theorem proves global forward continuation for a restricted class of expanding quantum–FLRW models with:

  • nonnegative coercive finite-mode potentials;

  • nonnegative cosmological constant;

  • expanding constraint data;

  • pure states in the exact operator domain.

This result establishes continuation of the specified homogeneous model. It does not establish vacuum stabilization, false-well protection, or a cosmological lifetime.

Matched dephasing intervention

A separate finite two-level register experiment examines a matched dephasing intervention with explicit detection branches and energy accounting.

Depending on the declared detuning, pulse timing, and observation window, the same type of intervention can either suppress or enhance leakage. The enhanced-leakage case includes a controller that extracts energy from the system; it is not interpreted as a generic heating mechanism.

The experiment demonstrates that measurement-like interventions do not possess a universal retention effect independent of dynamics and protocol. Transfer to the scalar false-well model would require a controlled metastable reduction, a physically realizable matched intervention, complete source accounting, and a defined detection protocol.

Derived memory-dependent coefficients

The paper derives reduced dynamical coefficients for the prescribed-background positive-quartic system–bath model rather than inserting a phenomenological decoherence rate.

The derivation includes:

  • an exact projection-memory identity;

  • nonequilibrium two-time bath correlations;

  • separate symmetric-noise and response kernels;

  • a second-order time-convolutionless generator;

  • an instantaneous signed decoherence matrix;

  • explicit memory dependence;

  • a fixed-cutoff approximation theorem;

  • a sufficient short-time condition for exact reduced-map invertibility.

No Markov, secular, thermal-equilibrium, or quantum-regression assumption is imposed.

The reduced coefficients depend on the full elapsed correlation history. They are therefore time-local in the evolving state argument but not memoryless. Their signed eigenvalues are derived quantities, not assumed positive constant rates.

Fixed-cutoff error theorem

For a finite parity-preserving matrix regularization with parity-invariant bath preparation, the paper proves a parity-improved fourth-order approximation bound between the exact reduced map and the second-order time-convolutionless propagator.

The bound:

  • holds at each fixed finite matrix cutoff;

  • applies to arbitrary system–ancilla inputs;

  • uses Dyson estimates and parity cancellation;

  • does not assume positivity of the approximate propagator;

  • does not require invertibility of the exact reduced map;

  • is conservative and may become numerically uninformative.

Because the bound depends on cutoff-dependent operator norms, it is not a uniform error theorem for the underlying unbounded-coordinate model. No cutoff-independent continuum master-equation theorem is claimed.

Weak-coupling numerical comparisons display the expected fourth-order scaling under coupling halving. At stronger coupling, the approximation develops negative eigenvalues and loses positivity, demonstrating its finite-regime failure boundary.

Relation to the published bath calculation

The paper also audits a published free-bath calculation concerning decoherence and false-vacuum decay. A printed exact free-bath mode is shown to fail the differential equation stated for it, and the residual, corrected solution and source-normalization issues are analyzed separately.

This correction is logically independent of the constructive theorem. It is not presented as a blanket refutation of the published physical proposal, nor does it supply a corrected continuum decay rate.

Scope and limitations

The paper establishes:

  • a local high-regularity quantum–classical-reference evolution;

  • explicit source accounting for matter, interaction, bath, counterterm, and reference sectors;

  • short-time initial entanglement;

  • a separate global homogeneous continuation theorem;

  • finite-time retention comparisons;

  • protocol-dependent suppression and enhancement of leakage;

  • an exact finite-memory reduced equation;

  • derived TCL2 coefficients;

  • a fixed-cutoff fourth-order approximation bound;

  • numerical identification of weak- and strong-coupling regimes.

It does not establish:

  • late-time false-vacuum protection;

  • a cosmological vacuum-decay lifetime;

  • a universal decoherence rate;

  • irreversible decoherence;

  • a positive Markov semigroup;

  • a cutoff-uniform unbounded-model estimate;

  • a renormalized interacting quantum-field limit;

  • quantized reference fields;

  • stochastic-gravity closure;

  • physical admission of the full upstream AASC source;

  • protection of the observed universe.

The paper’s mathematical conclusions are relative to the stated model classes, initial data, domains, calibration choices, cutoffs, and background assumptions.
