Complete publisher description
SYM-9 of the Standard Model Symmetry Arc Central Quotients, Genuine Line Operators, and Defect-Sector Normal Forms The local Standard Model gauge algebra and perturbative particle representations do not by themselves determine the global gauge theory. This paper classifies the compatible global forms of the Standard Model and reconstructs the associated line-operator, magnetic, topological, and generalized-symmetry structures. Fixing the local gauge algebra gSM=su(3)c⊕su(2)L⊕u(1)Y,\mathfrak{g}_{\mathrm{SM}} = \mathfrak{su}(3)_c \oplus \mathfrak{su}(2)_L \oplus \mathfrak{u}(1)_Y, the anomaly-neutral chiral matter and one-Higgs-doublet representation ledger, and an integer hypercharge convention, the common central element ξ=(ω3,−1,eiπ/3)\xi=(\omega_3,-1,e^{i\pi/3}) acts trivially on the inherited local matter package and generates a matter kernel isomorphic to Z6\mathbb{Z}_6. The compatible compact connected global-form targets are therefore GΓ=SU(3)c×SU(2)L×U(1)YΓ,Γ∈{1,Z2,Z3,Z6}.G_\Gamma = \frac{SU(3)_c\times SU(2)_L\times U(1)_Y}{\Gamma}, \qquad \Gamma\in\{1,\mathbb{Z}_2,\mathbb{Z}_3,\mathbb{Z}_6\}. For each global form, at fixed operator, magnetic, line-polarization, background-action, and phase scope, the paper reconstructs: electric descent conditions for Wilson lines; magnetic and dyonic charge lattices; Dirac–Schwinger–Zwanziger mutual locality; dynamical screening quotients; genuine electric and magnetic line classes; one-form symmetries; theta-identification lattices; Witten-effect line data; anomalous fermion-rephasing quotients; physical mass and Yukawa phase classes; electroweak fixation and electromagnetic line interfaces. The electric one-form factor is identified as Z6/Γ\mathbb{Z}_6/\Gamma, while the no-dynamical-monopole target carries a continuous magnetic U(1)(1)U(1)^{(1)}, with normalization and breaking determined by the admitted magnetic sector. The analysis distinguishes the gauge-topological theta torus ΘΓline=R3/Λθ,Γ;PΓ\Theta_\Gamma^{\mathrm{line}} = \mathbb{R}^3/\Lambda_{\theta,\Gamma;\mathcal{P}_\Gamma} from the full physical phase packet, which additionally includes mass and Yukawa phases modulo admitted anomalous fermion rephasings. This preserves Witten-effect and line-sector information without identifying a fixed matter-phase convention with the complete physical phase space. The paper demonstrates that distinct global completions can agree on all local perturbative correlators while differing in genuine line operators, line polarization, monopoles, screening, background actions, fractionalization data, topological response, theta identifications, and physical phase classes. They are therefore distinct completed targets whenever the operator category includes the corresponding observables. Two frontier structures are treated on explicitly qualified target fibers: continuous higher-flavor 2-group structure involving flavor backgrounds and the magnetic hypercharge one-form current; non-invertible axial symmetry defects in massless QED and first-generation massless QCD, including their action on ’t Hooft lines. These are not promoted into universal symmetries of the massive observed Standard Model. The resulting endpoint is a global-form-indexed defect-sector normal form and a precise handoff to SYM-11. Global and generalized-symmetry closure must be stated at a fixed global form and complete operator, background, and phase target, or explicitly across a family of completed targets. The paper does not claim to derive the experimentally realized global form, prove that Γ=Z6\Gamma=\mathbb{Z}_6 is uniquely selected, determine a unique maximal dyonic branch from the gauge group alone, classify every spin or non-spin refinement, determine numerical theta angles or couplings, or complete the entire Standard Model symmetry atlas. Principal contributions Classification of the compatible Standard Model global-form family. Derivation of the common Z6\mathbb{Z}_6 matter-center kernel. Electric Wilson-line descent and screening analysis. Magnetic and dyonic charge-lattice reconstruction. Dirac–Schwinger–Zwanziger mutual-locality constraints. Genuine line-operator and monopole-sector normal forms. One-form symmetry reconstruction. Gauge-topological theta identifications and Witten-effect analysis. Physical phase quotient including anomalous fermion rephasings. Electroweak and electromagnetic line-sector interface. Higher-group and non-invertible symmetry frontier fibers. Target-relative AASC classification and SYM-11 handoff.
