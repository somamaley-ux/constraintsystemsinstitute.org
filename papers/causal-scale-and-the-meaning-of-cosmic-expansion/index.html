Causal Scale and the Meaning of Cosmic Expansion

Material clocks, causal reach and the origin of light-element records

Amos Jay Maley · 100 pages

Read paperDownload PDF

Cosmic expansion is measurable in ruler units. A contracting metric can describe the same history when material standards, source dynamics and observations are transported together. The AMetric boundary supplies no external scale that would privilege either description.

What expansion measures

When the separation of the same comoving observers doubles in ruler units, both descriptions record that doubling. The expanding metric makes the calculation simpler. The contracting metric is a redescription on a regular matched interval, not a claim that the universe is really shrinking.

From clocks to light-element records

The paper constructs distinct clocks and a finite light record on one gravitating torus. On specified early-universe histories it follows the calibration through inflationary fluctuations, recombination and microwave polarization. Two modeled neutrino flavor preparations with the same initial geometry and flavor-summed occupations give different light-element yields. Each preparation gives the same yields in its two faithfully matched descriptions.

Scope

The light-element comparison tests the specified physical preparations and collision laws; it cannot distinguish two descriptions of the same history. The population link back to the inflationary plateau is conditional on the specified material interactions.
