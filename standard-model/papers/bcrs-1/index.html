Complete publisher description
Intrinsic Gauge Laws, Higgs Incidence, and Response Networks This paper establishes the foundational structural architecture of the Bosonic Carrier–Response–Projection Series (BCRS). It develops a premetric classification of the Standard Model’s bosonic response structure before metric, vacuum, particle, polarization, resonance, or confinement projection. The central distinction is between a carrier, the responses it supports, and the representations used to display those responses. These are treated as separate typed objects within a fixed premetric comparison target. The resulting framework prevents particle labels, generator counts, or anticipated physical boson assignments from entering the construction prematurely. A complete typed source grammar is introduced for the admissible response class. Its contextual signature records every defined, positive, null, and failure outcome after finite typed continuations. Equality of these complete signatures produces the canonical response quotient through which all licensed semantic uses and constructors factor uniquely, while preserving their exact domains of definition. Within this structure, the complete Standard Model source chain determines three gauge-response roots: charge response; color transport; pre-vacuum weak response. These are accompanied by one Higgs bridge/fixation identity. The Higgs sector is treated as a single carrier identity with distinct order, deformation, and fixation mechanisms rather than as multiple independent carriers. The paper then constructs a common source-realized fibre containing a mixed Higgs–electroweak readiness witness, structural residual charge transport, and a simultaneous compatible gauge assembly. On this source-realized domain, the jointly occupied carrier-root architecture has the separately typed invariant [ (3;1), ] representing three gauge-response roots and one Higgs carrier identity. This is a carrier-root result, not an undifferentiated count of all response classes, mechanism types, support structures, or eventual physical particles. The analysis further establishes: source descent and presentation-independent response comparison; fixed-input covariance for mixed and attached response instances; exact role eligibility and simultaneous compatibility; lawful response-assignment structure and its rigidity criterion; finite source-operation network representations preserving actual inputs, attachments, domains, and failure records; structural set-codescent on common response ledgers; rigidity of the complete compatible gauge-law reduct; retention of the positive Yang–Mills extended-support layer; a precise realization boundary for later metric, vacuum, polarization, resonance, and confinement constructions. A key result is the separation between law rigidity and response-value identity. The compatible gauge laws may be structurally fixed while distinct marked evaluations at different genuine source arguments remain distinguishable. Accordingly, the paper gives an exact criterion for any stronger singleton claim about fine-grained compatible response assignments without assuming that criterion in advance. The resulting BCRS-I object is a source-formed premetric response atlas: it records the carrier roots, Higgs identity, response quotient, mechanism fibres, transport structures, compatibility data, law networks, residual interfaces, and successor boundary required for subsequent physical realization. Scope and realization boundary The paper does not assert a physical particle count, polarization count, mass, width, pole, numerical vacuum value, numerical coupling, confinement result, asymptotic free-gluon state, graviton identity, or unrestricted physical common apex. Those conclusions require additional realization maps and their own antecedents. The result is therefore foundational rather than phenomenological: it identifies the structured bosonic architecture that must be preserved by later metric, vacuum, particle, resonance, and confinement analyses. Role in the BCRS programme BCRS-I supplies the premetric carrier–response foundation for the subsequent arc. Later papers may specialize this atlas into color, electroweak, Higgs, and physical boson realizations, but they must preserve the distinctions established here between carrier identity, response law, response evaluation, and physical projection.
