Complete publisher description
Joint Fermionic–Bosonic Classification, Shared-Source Coherence, and Physical Projection This paper establishes the structural spine of the constraint-derived Standard Model. It integrates the independently developed fermionic and bosonic classifications into one carrier–response–projection atlas, preserving their distinct mechanisms while reconstructing the shared source incidences through which matter, gauge, charge, Higgs, flavor, and physical responses interact. The central distinction is between: a complete source law; a carrier or root structure; a response evaluated at a particular argument; a physical realization or projection. Uniqueness of a source law does not imply uniqueness of every response value, and a response class does not automatically constitute an independently realized particle pole or physical state. The fermionic structure is received through the established three-root object [ |I_3|=3, ] together with its positioned matter-kind incidences, charge, chirality, color, Higgs-response, and generation-root architecture. These source-defined arrows precede familiar generation names, matrix dimensions, mass orderings, and physical particle labels. The bosonic structure is received through its complete gauge, charge, weak, color, and Higgs source families, including one canonical Higgs bridge with its order, deformation, fixation, guard, and relative-incidence data. The fermionic and bosonic sectors are not joined by merely matching names or taking a Cartesian product of marginal observables. Their common source is constructed from actual witnessed diagrams, shared interfaces, dependent domains, and retained attachment maps. A complete mixed operational algebra is formed before taking the observational quotient. It includes: fermionic occurrence and root maps; gauge transition, transport, loop, and obstruction operations; representation and charge responses; color-support and product-sector operations; Higgs order, deformation, and fixation; mixed current and tensor evaluations; relative-flavor and CP-sensitive operations; continuation, boundary, activity, and failure tests. The resulting mixed quotient is the greatest domain-reflecting observational congruence for the declared target. Its refinement beyond the separate fermionic and bosonic atlases is determined by the mixed-signature kernel. Exact partial domains, fixed sibling inputs, shared attachments, failure values, and independently formed continuations are retained throughout. The paper constructs a canonical common source supporting the simultaneous structural law. On its represented local-law branch, tensor probes recover the charged interaction coefficients and preserve relative flavor information. Complex traces of words of length at most seventeen classify pairs of three-dimensional Hermitian quark Gram matrices up to simultaneous unitary conjugacy, including singular and reducible cases. This finite reconstruction provides an explicit invariant treatment of relative flavor and CP information without selecting a preferred generation labeling. External root permutations are retained as marking transformations rather than treated as an identity quotient, and generation markings are distinguished from generation identities. The integrated role structure is then analyzed through eligibility, simultaneous compatibility, live residue, lawful assignments, and fixed-datum closure. The independently specified structural-law core is positively occupied and closed at each complete source datum, while stronger flavor, CP-response, terminal, and physical branches retain their own source requirements. The atlas also establishes: source reception before quotient formation; fixed-input covariance and intrinsic sibling preservation; finite decorated-network normal forms; exact definedness and failure tracking; typed process composition; source-relative continuation; reconstruction of the complete mixed response structure; an exact no-repair deletion criterion; conservative passage to later physical realizations; exhaustion of additional same-scope label authority. Conventional particle names are therefore recovered through typed projection and marking records rather than used to define the identities they denote. The premetric gravity role is retained as a condition of coherent physical standing and continuation, while metric, classical, quantum, spectral, pole, confinement, and asymptotic structures enter only through their licensed source and authority maps. Scope and realization boundary The paper establishes a branch-complete structural classification and reconstruction theorem for the received Standard Model source target. It does not select numerical parameters, a preferred vacuum, a unique physical history, or an unrestricted common physical realization. Nonzero represented interaction operations are not automatically nonzero on-shell fermion histories or quantum states. The received classical gauge–Higgs–gravity branch retains its own source conditions and does not replace the fermionic occurrence witnesses with a stronger populated classical solution. Likewise, physical particle poles, confinement, interacting quantum-field realizations, complete asymptotic sectors, and an unrestricted physical common apex require their own compatible source authorities. A later projection cannot revise an earlier source identity or fill a missing upstream witness without an explicitly declared extension. Role in the AASC programme CSPS-S is the structural-spine paper for the constraint-derived Standard Model. It integrates the FCGS fermionic root and generation structure, the BCRS bosonic response architecture, the common Higgs source, mixed observational quotient, role-occupancy closure, and typed physical-projection frontier. Its principal result is a coherent carrier–response–projection classification in which fermionic differentiation, gauge response, Higgs fixation, flavor structure, gravity, and physical realization share one source without being collapsed into one undifferentiated object.
